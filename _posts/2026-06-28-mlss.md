In June 2026, I had the privledge to attend the [Machine Learning Summer School](https://cfe.columbia.edu/content/mlss2) hosted by Columbia University, New York, USA for two weeks. Having nearly finished my second year, this was a great opportunity to learn about some of the latest research topics puzzling the minds of academcis and industry personal alike.

![Columbia Campus](/images/ColumbiaBlog/campus.png)
*Columbia Library*

## About the Program

I was one of around 200 students (PhD, Master's and Post Docs) selected out of the 1,200 who applied to take part in the program. It was a two-week intesive program, with lectures everyday from 9am-5pm, discussing topics such as causal machine learning, time-series, reinforcement learning and agents, many of which I had little to no prior understanding about. We were given accomodation at Columbia's main campus, located in upper West Manhattan, which brought back many memories of entering my first-year undergraduate halls!

The Columbia campus was honestly stunning. Apart from maybe Oxford and Cambridge, I don't know if I have seen as beatiful a campus. The campus was quite busy for being off-term and it was great to explore such a historic educational institution. 

## Learning Highlights
There were some greats talks and insights throughout the two weeks. Some of the lecturers were academic professors whilst others were industry leaders at places such as Bloomberg (who were the sponsors of the event). Here are a few highlights that really stuck out to me:

- Having spent most of my PhD training transoformer models from scratch, I was very excited to learn about masked discrete diffusion language models from one of the leaders in the field, [Volodymyr Kuleshov](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~kuleshov/). His talk was probably my highlight of the program as he managed to capture the fundamental reason why this avenue is worth exploring, how you can actually train in a smooth manner and some of the challenges with this objective. I will (and have) definitely be exploring more applications of discrete diffusion and if you want some more understanding, I would reccomend his blog on [how to train a diffusion language model](https://kuleshov-group.github.io/blog/blog/2026/how-to-build-a-diffusion-language-model/).
- Professor [Kyunghyun Cho](https://kyunghyuncho.me/) is one of the most cited academics in the world, having worked on deep learning extensivley. He presented his work on meta-learning, where he showed how he has been building models to predict different properties of distributions such as standard deviation or mutual information that would otherwise be a biased or intractable formulation. Although I did not understand his talk completely, to see someone who has shaped the field in such a postive way was incredible and the way he conveyed his points was with such purpose. 
- I have always been interested in time-series data having done many signal processing modules during my undergraduate, and seeing how both traditional machine learning techniques, such as linear regression, as well as foundation models are being applied to financial data was super interesting.
- Mechananistic Interpretability is a field I became aware of recently after reading some of [Anthropic's blog](https://transformer-circuits.pub/). The idea is to break down a circuit within a model and understand how changing certain layers, weights or neurons can influence the model, and [Michael Ivanitsky](https://miv.name/) did a great job at conveying where this field is heading. This is definitely a topic I will be exploring within my healthcare work.
- Although I am not super interested in this work, there is something to be said about developing agents. Agents can help us complete many of the mundane tasks and focus on answering the real scientific questions. the main question is 'How can we make sure the agent behaves reliably?'


## New York

Having lived in London for the last 7 years, I have become pretty used to big cities, but New York took it to a whole other level and I loved it. I think I visited New York at maybe the best time ever. Being at the end of June, the weather was sunny everyday without being too humid, the Knicks had just won the NBA championship for the first time in 53 years and the FIFA World Cup was being hosted. I was lucky enough to explore toursits ares such as the West Villiage, Dumbo and Williamsburg. Some of my highlights are:

- Cetnral Park: Columbia's campus is actually located in a pretty quiet part of Manhattan and one of the highlights was it's proximity to Central Park. I thought that the park would be very similar to Hyde Park, so was surprised to see the dense vegetation and hills. I made use of the jet-lag and managed to go for three early morning runs. The energy was unreal, especially on the weekend, when you have hundreds of runners jogging around the park in the designated running paths. Later on in the second week, I would go for a cycle around the park and watch different familes play on the grass and teams play in their softball or kickball competitions. 
- Magnolia's bakery: I know this is on everyone's list, but the banana pudding here is one of the tastiest things I have every had
- The 9/11 Museum: I think everyone should visit this museum if they visit the city. You could spend hours here with very touching exhibitions throughout. They have done a great job at conveying the chaos, terror and sadness of that day as well as how people were personally affected. The photos of people jumping from the burning building were particularly harrowing and it is hard not to think about the tradegy of that day when walking around the financial district. 
- Tacos: Living in the UK, I think I have realised that we cannot access tasty Mexican food. One day, my home friend, girlfriend and I watched England vs Paraguay at a bar in Williamsburg and got some tacos after, a memory I'll never forget. 
- Pizza crawl: My girlfriend and I did a pizza crawl around the West Village, stopping at places like L'industrie and Joe's Pizza before going on to get some cocktails. It's fun to act the tourist sometimes!

Overall the experience was amazing. I met some great researchers, an appreciation for the depth and breadth of the field and got to explore one of the greatest cities. I met some great people who I plan on being in touch with and if you ever have the chance to complete one of MLSS courses then I would 100% reccomend.
